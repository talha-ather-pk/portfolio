HR Analytics & Employee Attrition

A Power BI dashboard that shows who is leaving, from where, and at what pay level, built so an HR manager can answer attrition questions without opening a spreadsheet.

Employees analysed
1,470
Attrition
237 leavers (16.12%)
Tools
Power BI, Power Query, DAX
Data
IBM HR Attrition dataset

The problem

Attrition is expensive, but raw HR exports only list employees one row at a time. Management needs to see at a glance where people are leaving and which groups are most at risk, and to filter by department, gender and job role.

Data sources

The public IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition dataset (WA_Fn-UseC_-HR-Employee-Attrition): one row per employee, with age, department, job role, business travel, monthly income, overtime, years at company and an attrition flag.

Dashboard

Approach

1. Prepare the data in Power Query. Promoted headers, set column types, removed fields the analysis didn't need, and added conditional columns to group employees into bands such as age group and salary band.

2. Build the measures in DAX. Total Employees, Active Employees, Attrition Count, Attrition Rate, Average Age, Average Monthly Income and Average Years at Company, all in a dedicated measures table.

3. Check the numbers. Ran the measures in DAX query view to confirm the totals (1,470 employees, 237 attrition, 1,233 active) before building visuals on top of them.

4. Design for the question being asked. KPI cards across the top, breakdowns by department, gender, job role, salary band, overtime and age group below, and slicers so a manager can drill into one department or role.

Tools

Power BI Desktop, Power Query (M), DAX

Outcome

  • Overall attrition is 16.12%: 237 of 1,470 employees.
  • Research & Development has the most leavers (133), followed by Sales (92) and Human Resources (12).
  • The low salary band accounts for the most attrition (113 leavers), almost double the medium band (66).
  • Employees aged 25–34 are the largest group in the workforce (554).

Lessons learned

1–2 honest lessons, e.g. counts vs rates: R&D has the most leavers, but it is also the biggest department. Attrition rate by department would show which team actually loses people fastest.
